# Pilot observations and next questions

These notes are provisional prompts generated from a purposive 10-case dataset. They are not prevalence findings and should not be presented as what Kenyan courts “generally” do.

## 1. Technical objects acquire legal meaning through context

An IMEI, phone recording, authentication certificate, broadcast signal, or identity database is not legally self-explanatory. The coded decisions repeatedly require a court to ask what the object does, how it is linked to people, who controls it, and what legal rule attaches to that relationship.

**Next question:** Can the Status and Proof stages reduce errors caused by treating a technical label as a complete account of the system?

## 2. Safeguards appear in both merits and remedies

In the surveillance and digital-identity cases, safeguards are not only abstract principles. Courts also express them as prior judicial control, regulation, public participation, a DPIA, implementation conditions, or a suspended declaration.

**Next question:** Should the Lens distinguish legal validity safeguards from implementation and closure-proof safeguards?

## 3. Electronic evidence is a chain, not a single file

The two electronic-evidence decisions expose several stages: capture, device control, copying, certification, identification, admission, challenge, and weight. A certificate may authenticate one stage but not another.

**Next question:** Would a provenance diagram make section 106B analysis more transparent without oversimplifying judicial discretion?

## 4. Legitimate aims do not settle proportionality

Counterterrorism, counterfeit-device control, reputation protection, public information access, and cybercrime regulation can be legitimate aims. The cases still examine authority, precision, necessity, safeguards, and less restrictive means.

**Next question:** How should a structured method keep the public aim visible while still red-teaming overbreadth and rights risk?

## 5. Appellate and implementation status are part of legal accuracy

A statement of a holding can be textually accurate but practically misleading if an appeal, stay, suspended declaration, regulatory response, or later related judgment is omitted.

**Next question:** What minimum status fields should be mandatory before a case proposition is used in a dissertation, scholarship application, or legal memorandum?
