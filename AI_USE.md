# Responsible AI use statement

## Human responsibility

Moses Evans Etyang Oroni is the project owner and remains responsible for the research questions, inclusion decisions, interpretations, errors, revisions, and any academic use of this work.

## How AI assisted v0.1

AI assistance was used to help:

- organise the repository and convert an existing research framework into a public release;
- structure the case-coding fields and draft concise case summaries;
- identify contradictions, privacy risks, and overclaiming;
- check internal consistency across documentation; and
- improve wording, formatting, and accessibility.

AI output was **not** treated as legal authority. Case citations, dates, courts, outcomes, and source links were checked against Kenya Law. Any later reuse must repeat that verification because law and appellate status can change.

## Controls

1. **Primary-source rule:** legal propositions must link to an official or otherwise authoritative source.
2. **Human review:** no AI-generated case summary enters a release without a human-owned verification step.
3. **Uncertainty labels:** unresolved appellate status, incomplete verification, and inference must be stated rather than hidden.
4. **Data minimisation:** private client, participant, programme, KYC, health, financial, security, and correspondence data are excluded.
5. **No automated legal advice:** this project supports research and explanation; it does not make legal decisions for people.
6. **Academic disclosure:** substantial AI assistance must be disclosed under the rules of the relevant university, journal, funder, or scholarship programme.
7. **Reproducibility:** the source, retrieval date, coding method, and version are recorded so another researcher can challenge or repeat the work.

## Known limitations

Large-language models can fabricate citations, compress important distinctions, miss later appeals, and reproduce bias. A fluent summary is not proof of accuracy. The project therefore treats AI as a fallible research aid operating inside a source-verification and human-accountability process.
