# Legal Lens framework

## Six-question test

| Step | Question | Minimum output |
|---|---|---|
| Status | What is true now, and on what date? | Dated fact statement; known/unknown boundary |
| Authority | What source can validly establish it? | Jurisdiction, institution, document owner, and source location |
| Duty | What legal, ethical, contractual, or governance obligation applies? | Duty holder, beneficiary, trigger, and consequence |
| Proof | What proves compliance, performance, or outcome? | Document, reference, consent, receipt, or verified result |
| Risk | What is missing, contradictory, stale, sensitive, or misleading? | Risk statement, affected person, and control |
| Action | What is the smallest useful next step? | Owner, deadline, closure proof, and consent status |

## Evidence states

- **A — Current and authoritative:** primary law, regulator or institution record, controlling agreement, or direct current account record.
- **B — Current and corroborated:** recent working material supported by another current source.
- **C — Current but unconfirmed:** plausible and recent, but awaiting primary proof.
- **D — Historical only:** useful context that cannot establish present status.
- **Q — Quarantined conflict:** contradictory or materially risky until reconciled.

## Core rules

1. Date every proposition capable of changing.
2. State jurisdiction and authority for legal propositions.
3. Separate fact, inference, recommendation, and personal meaning.
4. Preserve contradictions until a stronger source resolves them.
5. Treat access as access—not evidence, authority, competence, or consent.
6. Minimise personal and sensitive data before analysis or publication.
7. Attach an owner, deadline, and closure test to every operational recommendation.
8. Put consequential action through the consent gate.
9. Use Desk Review for ambiguity, significant risk, repeating patterns, and learning.
10. Version the result and record what changed.

## Research proposition

The framework is provisional. The dissertation would test whether this explicit sequence improves transparency, source discipline, rights sensitivity, and error containment in selected Kenyan technology-law problems. The project does not assume that the framework is novel, superior, judicially accepted, or academically validated.
