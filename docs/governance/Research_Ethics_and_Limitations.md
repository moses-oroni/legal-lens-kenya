# Research ethics and limitations

## Ethics position

The v0.1 release analyses public judgments and publishes no participant research. Any later interviews, surveys, case studies involving people, or use of non-public organisational records should begin only after the applicable university ethics and institutional permissions are identified and obtained.

## Limitations

1. **Small purposive sample:** ten cases can test a workflow but cannot establish the frequency or prevalence of a reasoning pattern across Kenyan law.
2. **Selection bias:** the cases were chosen for doctrinal and technology diversity; prominent reported cases are more likely to be included.
3. **Compression:** a single CSV row cannot reproduce the full facts, submissions, reasoning, separate opinions, or remedial context of a judgment.
4. **Changing law:** appeals, stays, legislation, regulation, and implementation can alter a case's practical significance.
5. **Researcher interpretation:** issue labels, reasoning steps, evidence gaps, and Lens observations involve judgment and are open to challenge.
6. **AI fallibility:** AI-assisted drafting may omit distinctions or introduce error even where the prose appears confident.
7. **No validated intervention:** the Legal Lens has not been empirically shown to improve legal outcomes, access to justice, or institutional performance.
8. **No legal advice:** the release is for research and portfolio demonstration, not advice on a live matter.

## Harm minimisation

- Quote only what is necessary.
- Avoid amplifying intimate, identifying, or traumatic facts from public cases.
- Prefer anonymised references where the official report is anonymised.
- Separate legal evaluation from claims about the character or credibility of affected people.
- Invite correction and preserve a visible change log.

## Claims this release does not make

The release does not claim that:

- Kenyan courts currently use the Legal Lens;
- the framework is doctrinally required;
- the sample establishes a national judicial trend;
- AI reasoning is equivalent to legal professional judgment; or
- a university, supervisor, court, funder, or professional body has approved the project.
