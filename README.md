# Legal Lens Kenya — AI Accountability

**Researcher:** Moses Evans Etyang Oroni  
**Direction adopted:** 7 September 2026  
**Research version:** 0.2.0 — exploratory

Legal Lens investigates criminal accountability when prompts, AI agents or programs contribute to digital harm in Kenya. Its first focus is **fraud and unauthorised access**.

**Working dissertation title:** *Criminal Accountability for AI-Mediated Cybercrime in Kenya: Assessing the Adequacy of the Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act.*

## The question
When AI carries out a harmful act, who instructed, controlled, authorised or manipulated it—and what admissible evidence connects each person to the elements of an offence?

The project tests whether a problem is a substantive legal gap, an interpretive question, an evidential obstacle or an enforcement problem. The absence of the words “AI” or “prompt engineering” does not itself prove a legislative gap.

## Practical use
An incident assessment records the timeline, actors, authority to act, candidate offence elements, missing evidence, alternative explanations and bounded human-review questions. It does not determine guilt, assign liability percentages or claim that software is a criminal legal person.

The existing six-stage discipline remains:
**Status → Authority → Duty → Proof → Risk → Action.**

## Evidence and research status
This is a research direction and prototype specification, not a completed finding that Kenyan law is inadequate. The title and design remain subject to university and supervisor requirements. No claim of novelty, legal validation or market demand has been established.

The original [ten-case pilot](data/kenya_digital_law_case_pilot_v0.1.csv) remains historical contextual material. It is not an AI-crime dataset and was not reverified during this direction change. Fictional scenarios are labelled and kept separate from judgments.

Legal status requires the Act as amended, commencement information and later court orders. Located source links are not equivalent to full-text verification. Unresolved authorities must not support definitive conclusions.
